Scientific Landscape

Conceptual framework for movement organization, multisystem response and whole-organism adaptation

From Movement Organization to Whole-Organism Adaptation: An Integrated Framework for Multisystem Physiological Research

SCIENTIFIC LANDSCAPE

From individual components to interacting biological systems

Research on human movement commonly examines specific anatomical, physiological, neuromuscular, biomechanical or functional characteristics. These approaches provide important information about defined components of human movement and biological function.

At the same time, whole-organism physiology, systems biology and related approaches emphasize interactions among biological systems and the dynamic behaviour that emerges from those interactions.

Network Physiology provides an additional conceptual perspective in which physiological systems are examined as dynamically interacting components whose coordination can change across states and time scales.

The present framework builds on these perspectives without treating movement observations as direct substitutes for physiological measurements or established biological mechanisms.

CONCEPTUAL POSITION

Movement as an observable expression of whole-organism state

The framework places movement at an observable level between an organism's current state and the multisystem response that follows a movement impulse.

01

Current biological state

Each movement episode occurs within a dynamic internal state shaped by ongoing biological, environmental and contextual conditions.

STATE
02

Movement impulse

A temporally bounded movement episode provides the immediate event through which movement organization can be observed and operationalized.

INPUT
03

Movement organization

Spatial, temporal, mechanical and neuromuscular characteristics define how the movement episode is organized.

CORE CONSTRUCT
04

Multisystem response

Prespecified physiological variables can be examined as a temporally structured response across relevant biological domains.

RESPONSE
05

Recovery and remodelling

The post-movement response is characterized across time, including recovery dynamics and return toward the individual's subsequent state.

RECOVERY
06

Adaptation

Repeated exposure is examined for reproducible within-person changes in response characteristics and longer-term trajectories.

DOWNSTREAM TARGET
MOVEMENT QUALITY

A multidimensional and context-dependent concept

Movement quality is not defined in this framework as a universally prescribed biomechanical ideal.

It is treated as a multidimensional and context-dependent set of observable characteristics whose relevance must be evaluated in relation to the conditions under which movement occurs.

Candidate characteristics may include consistency, variability, coordination, timing, stability and adaptability to changing conditions.

These characteristics remain descriptive and operational until empirical evidence supports stronger interpretation.

ANALYTICAL DISTINCTIONS

Observation, measurement and biological interpretation

Observation and measurement

Movement characteristics constitute the observable component of the framework and should be operationally defined and measured using reproducible procedures.

Repeated observations under defined conditions allow consistency, variability and changes in movement organization to be characterized.

Interpretation

Biological explanations represent a separate interpretive level. An observed movement change may be associated with changes in one or more underlying systems, but movement observation alone does not establish a specific mechanism.

Maintaining this distinction is necessary for empirical testing and for avoiding premature biological attribution.

CONDITIONS AND CONTEXT

Movement characteristics must be interpreted within defined conditions

Movement organization is context-dependent. The same individual may demonstrate different movement patterns when task demands, environmental conditions or other relevant constraints change.

Repeated measurement under comparable conditions provides information about consistency and variability, while comparison across defined conditions provides an opportunity to examine stability and adaptability.

The framework therefore treats condition definition and repeated observation as essential elements of meaningful movement characterization.

RELATIONSHIP

ITERATION
SCOPE

What this framework does not claim

The framework does not assume that movement quality is a direct or specific biomarker of biological adaptation.

It does not propose that movement observations replace established physiological diagnostics, risk assessment, medical treatment or rehabilitation.

The proposed relationships remain hypotheses until appropriate measurement, reproducibility analysis and empirical validation provide sufficient evidence.
