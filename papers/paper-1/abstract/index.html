PAPER 1 · ABSTRACT

Beyond Homeostasis and Allostasis: Is Continuous Whole-Organism Coordination an Underdefined Biological Phenomenon?

Conceptual analysis of continuous organism-wide coordination within contemporary biological terminology.

Draft v0.5 · Conceptual manuscript

ABSTRACT

Background

Biological organisms are characterized by coordinated activity across multiple interacting levels and systems. Contemporary biology and physiology provide established concepts for describing organismal structure, regulation, homeostasis, allostasis, information transfer, signaling, and communication. However, it remains less clear whether current terminology explicitly defines continuous whole-organism coordination as a distinct conceptual phenomenon.

Objective

To determine whether continuous organism-wide coordination is explicitly represented within current biological terminology or whether this phenomenon remains conceptually distributed across multiple partially overlapping concepts without a unified definition.

Methods

The investigation was conceptual rather than experimental. A structured Concept Audit framework was applied to established biological concepts across multiple disciplinary traditions. The analysis examined the definitions, explanatory scope, organizational level, and limitations of selected concepts relevant to organismal structure, regulation, information, signaling, and communication. No laboratory experiments, physiological measurements, statistical analyses, or clinical interventions were performed.

Results

The audited concepts provided rigorous explanations for specific aspects of biological organization, including organismal structure, regulatory processes, physiological stability, adaptive regulation, information transfer, signaling, and communication. However, no concept was found to explicitly define continuous whole-organism coordination as an independent conceptual entity.

This finding reflects the current structure of biological terminology rather than evidence for the biological existence or absence of such a phenomenon.

Conclusions

The analysis identifies a conceptually underdefined area at the intersection of established descriptions of organismal structure, regulation, information, and communication. The findings do not establish a new biological theory. Instead, they provide a conceptual foundation for future empirical investigation of whether continuous whole-organism coordination can be operationally characterized and measured.

POSITIONING

Conceptual rather than empirical

Paper 1 is intentionally limited to a conceptual examination of biological terminology.

The absence of an explicit unified definition is not interpreted as evidence that the underlying biological phenomenon exists or does not exist.

The purpose of the study is to identify whether a sufficiently defined conceptual area exists to justify subsequent empirical investigation.
