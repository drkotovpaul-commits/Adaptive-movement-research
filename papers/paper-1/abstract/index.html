Movement quality as a systems-level indicator

Adaptive Movement Research

ABSTRACT

Research premise

This paper investigates whether measurable properties of human movement can provide useful information about biological adaptation at the systems level.

The central premise is that movement should not be considered solely as the visible output of individual anatomical or physiological components. Instead, movement quality may reflect the interaction of multiple regulatory, mechanical, sensory and adaptive processes operating across the organism.

The research therefore focuses on movement quality as a potentially integrative observable variable. Particular attention is given to consistency, variability, coordination and changes in movement characteristics under different conditions.

The objective of the research program is to establish a coherent framework for describing these properties, identifying measurable variables and determining which aspects of movement may provide meaningful information about adaptive state.

The present manuscript establishes the conceptual foundation for this research program. Subsequent sections will address the research framework, methodology, empirical findings, interpretation and bibliographic foundation.
