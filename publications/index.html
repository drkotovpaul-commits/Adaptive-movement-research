CURRENT PUBLICATION RECORD

A transparent record of research outputs

Adaptive Movement Research currently has two released public preprints representing successive stages of the programme's conceptual development.

The released preprints are presented as fixed public scholarly records, while continuing manuscript development and future peer-reviewed publication remain separate stages. These categories are kept distinct so that preprints, evolving manuscripts and peer-reviewed articles are not treated as equivalent publication states.

RELEASED PREPRINT

Closed-Loop Movement–State Coupling

Toward Closed-Loop Movement-State Coupling: An Integrative Framework for Multisystem Response, Recovery, and Adaptive Movement Exposure

This second AMR preprint develops the programme beyond the foundational framework by formalizing repeated movement–state transitions through measured state, movement-derived output, multisystem response, recovery and incremental prediction.

The central empirical question is whether this integrated representation provides reproducible short-horizon predictive value beyond credible simpler comparators.

Prospective movement-exposure selection is positioned as a downstream step requiring separate predictive validation rather than as an already validated intervention strategy.

PUBLICATION POLICY

Publication status is reported by evidence stage

Adaptive Movement Research distinguishes between a manuscript in development, a released preprint, a submitted manuscript and a formally peer-reviewed publication.

A preprint establishes a public scholarly record but does not constitute peer-reviewed acceptance.

Journal submission and editorial evaluation are publication- process events rather than evidence of scientific validation.

Journal-specific publication records will be added only when the corresponding scholarly output reaches formal acceptance and publication.

CURRENT PROGRAMME

Beyond the foundational publication

Adaptive Movement Research has progressed beyond the initial conceptual Framework Paper into repeated state-transition modelling, operationalization and empirical development.

The current programme examines measured biological state, movement-derived output, multisystem response, recovery, repeated state transitions and adaptation while developing an empirical framework for prospective testing.

These developments are recorded as current research architecture and should not be interpreted as findings established by the released preprints.
