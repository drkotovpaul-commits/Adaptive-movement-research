RESEARCH PROGRAM

Research

Movement quality as a potential systems-level indicator of human biological adaptation

Adaptive Movement Research is an independent research program investigating whether measurable properties of human movement can provide information about biological adaptation at the systems level.

View Papers Research Framework
RESEARCH QUESTION

Can movement quality provide systems-level information?

Human movement is an integrated outcome of interacting biological, neuromuscular, sensory and mechanical processes.

The research program investigates whether measurable characteristics of movement can contain information about changes occurring within these interacting systems.

The central question is therefore not whether movement can replace established physiological or anatomical measurements, but whether movement quality can provide an additional observable layer of information about biological adaptation.

RESEARCH LOGIC

A measurement-first approach

The research program maintains a deliberate distinction between what is observed, what is measured and what is inferred.

Candidate movement characteristics must first be operationally defined and evaluated for reproducibility. Their behaviour across repeated observations and changing conditions can then be examined.

Only after empirical validation can relationships between movement characteristics and biological adaptation be considered with greater confidence.
